# QE01: Repeated-seed Monte Carlo convergence

## Question

For the existing synthetic European call, how do plain and paired-antithetic
Monte Carlo RMSE and empirical 95% interval coverage change with equal payoff
budgets? A single seed cannot answer this question reliably.

## Method and cost assumptions

The option uses spot = strike = 100, annual risk-free rate = 0.03, volatility =
0.20, maturity = 1 year, and no dividends. All observations are **synthetic**
risk-neutral geometric Brownian motion terminal draws, with exact terminal
sampling rather than time stepping. The Black-Scholes analytic reference is
**9.413403383853016**. See the [original pricing paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/260062).

Each method has 128 independent pseudorandom replicates at each of four budgets:
256, 1,024, 4,096, and 16,384 discounted-payoff evaluations. For budget N, plain
sampling uses N normals; antithetic sampling uses N/2 normals and evaluates both
signs, averaging within each pair. Its standard error uses the N/2 independent
pair means. This equal-payoff cost model excludes normal generation, memory,
wall-clock time, and all quantum oracle/state-preparation costs. The repeated
study performs 5,570,560 payoff evaluations; the preserved single-seed baseline
adds another 16,384.

Root seed 1729 spawns 1,024 child SeedSequences in budget/method/replicate order.
Each child supplies one uint64 seed, recorded in the artifact and replayable
through `monte_carlo(paths=N, seed=recorded_seed, antithetic=...)`. Methods and
budgets use separate streams; samples are not nested or shared. Independence is
the practical pseudorandom guarantee documented by [NumPy](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/parallel.html),
not a proof of independent physical randomness. Changing the budget list or
replicate count changes seed allocation. No seed was selected or removed after
seeing results.

For replicate errors e_i relative to the analytic price, RMSE is
sqrt(mean(e_i squared)) and bias is mean(e_i). Coverage is the fraction of
replicate intervals containing that price, including endpoints. Each interval
is estimate +/- 1.96 times its sample standard error (sample variance uses
ddof=1). These approximate intervals and antithetic averaging follow standard
[Monte Carlo methodology](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4336/monte_carlo_methods_security_pricing.pdf),
pp. 188-190. The artifact also reports average standard error, interval width,
and coverage Monte Carlo standard error sqrt(p_hat*(1-p_hat)/128).

## Actual findings

Values below come from the regenerated artifact; each coverage denominator is 128.
RMSE is in option-price units.

| Payoff budget | Plain RMSE | Paired-antithetic RMSE | Plain coverage | Paired-antithetic coverage |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 256 | 0.867514 | 0.732290 | 121/128 = 94.53% | 115/128 = 89.84% |
| 1,024 | 0.444640 | 0.330743 | 119/128 = 92.97% | 120/128 = 93.75% |
| 4,096 | 0.218188 | 0.158094 | 120/128 = 93.75% | 124/128 = 96.88% |
| 16,384 | 0.098050 | 0.087051 | 127/128 = 99.22% | 119/128 = 92.97% |

RMSE decreased for both methods across this recorded grid. Antithetic RMSE was
lower at all four budgets, but the size of that difference varied. This is an
observation for this experiment, not a formal significance test or universal
performance guarantee.

Coverage was not monotone in budget and did not consistently match 95%.
In particular, paired-antithetic coverage was 89.84% at 256 paths and 92.97%
at 16,384 paths. Plain coverage reached 99.22% at the largest budget. These
negative calibration findings are retained. At the smallest budget, the coverage
Monte Carlo standard errors were 2.01 percentage points (plain) and 2.67 points
(antithetic); finite replicate uncertainty matters. No persistent undercoverage
or overcoverage conclusion follows from these eight cells alone.

## Limitations

Only one synthetic option setting was studied; no market data, calibration,
transaction costs, dividends, or model misspecification are represented. The
128 replicates give noisy RMSE and coverage estimates; no RMSE uncertainty
intervals or multiplicity-adjusted calibration tests were computed. Plug-in
coverage standard errors are descriptive and can be unreliable near zero or
one. Normal intervals are asymptotic, with no finite-sample coverage guarantee.
No runtime measurements, quantum computation, novel estimator, or quantum
advantage are claimed. Reproduction should use the recorded library versions;
future RNG or numerical-library changes may affect exact values.

## What the author should understand for an interview

- Explain why the analytic price is a reference under this model, not observed
  market truth, and why exact GBM terminal sampling avoids time-discretization bias.
- Derive the pair-mean variance, including covariance of the two payoffs, and
  explain why treating all antithetic payoffs as independent gives the wrong SE.
- Distinguish a within-run standard error from across-seed RMSE, bias, and
  coverage. A narrow interval can miss the truth; a good point estimate does not
  establish interval calibration.
- Explain the usual inverse-square-root Monte Carlo error scaling, why a finite
  RMSE grid need not decrease monotonically, and why this grid alone does not
  estimate an asymptotic rate reliably.
- Defend equal payoff evaluations as an explicit cost assumption and describe
  which additional costs and evidence a runtime or quantum advantage claim needs.

The acceptance criteria are met: both estimators, 128 independent pseudorandom
seeds per cell, four path budgets, and measured RMSE and interval coverage
against the analytic price. This milestone adds experimental evidence to an
existing classical baseline.
