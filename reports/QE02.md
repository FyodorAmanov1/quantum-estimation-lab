# QE02: Calibrated portfolio scenarios

## Question

How much do the existing option book's risk metrics change when its frozen
synthetic volatility and correlation assumptions are replaced by estimates
from a dated price CSV? How much of the total difference is simply a spot-price
change? Calibration is evaluated as a sensitivity experiment, not as evidence
of market accuracy.

## Method

The existing four contracts, units, strikes, 21/252-year horizon, six-month
initial maturity and 3% rate are held fixed. The CSV adapter now feeds the same
correlated GBM and Black–Scholes revaluation engine used by the legacy cases.
Column names A, B, C, D identify positions independently of column order.
Other asset sets fail explicitly. The original public calls and default
scenario results remain available.

The adapter requires a `date` header, unique asset names, strictly ascending
ISO dates, positive finite complete prices and at least 60 included closes.
User comparisons require an explicit as-of date. Rows after the cutoff never
enter the fitted spots or covariance. Both requested cutoff and actual window
end are recorded, allowing stale inputs to be identified. The entire source
file is fingerprinted with SHA-256, so even excluded-row changes alter the
fingerprint while leaving fitted results unchanged. Raw user prices and local
input paths are not exported.

For daily log returns, sample covariance uses the N−1 divisor, then multiplies
by 252 and applies 90% sample covariance plus 10% its diagonal. Volatilities
are square roots of the diagonal; correlations normalize the covariance.
See the [NumPy definition](https://numpy.org/doc/1.20/reference/generated/numpy.cov.html).
The shrinkage intensity is a fixed regularization choice, not an optimized
estimate; [Ledoit and Wolf (2004)](https://ledoit.net/Honey_2004.pdf) give primary
background on shrinkage, rather than validation of this particular choice.

Three models receive identical scrambled Sobol normal draws:

1. Original frozen synthetic assumptions, labeled as of 2025-12-31. This date
   labels illustrative assumptions; it does not identify historical quotes.
2. Spot-matched baseline: CSV final spots, frozen volatilities and correlations.
3. Calibrated: the same CSV spots, with fitted volatilities and correlations.

The last two models isolate the joint volatility/correlation change. Each
model uses its own initial option value under its parameters; the comparison
therefore includes both scenario and pricing-model sensitivity, not solely
terminal-return sensitivity. Historical volatility supplies pricing volatility
as an explicit proxy. No historical mean-return drift is fitted.

The bundled input is entirely **synthetic**, generated by
`examples/generate_prices.py` with seed 202502, independent Student-t(df=5)
innovations scaled to unit variance before correlation, and fixed annual
volatility scales [0.30, 0.20, 0.26, 0.40]. Correlations before fitting follow
0.7 raised to asset-index distance. There are 253 weekday closes from
2025-01-13 through 2025-12-31; weekdays are not an exchange calendar. Fitting
uses 252 returns. No market dataset, network retrieval or credentials are needed.
The Gaussian fitted engine intentionally does not reproduce the input's tail
shape. A comparison against a non-Gaussian scenario model remains future work.

Each main model uses 32,768 paths with scramble seed 1729. Twelve additional
paired comparisons use seeds 100–111. Thus the calibration study evaluates
27 × 32,768 = 884,736 book paths, each revaluing four contracts, plus three
main and 24 replicate initial-book valuations. Costs exclude wall time,
calibration work, random generation and all quantum-oracle costs.

VaR is the linearly interpolated empirical 99% loss quantile; ES averages
observations at or above that threshold. Loss is initial value minus discounted
future book value. PFE is the 99% quantile of positive, undiscounted future
book value. Standard deviation across paired scramble differences and its
standard error divided by sqrt(12) describe numerical variability only.

## Reproduce

Using the pinned requirements, from the repository root:

```text
python examples/generate_prices.py
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python lab.py --output results/latest.json
python src/studio.py --out docs/study.json
python src/studio.py --check docs/study.json
```

For a user CSV, preserve its provenance and adjustment conventions and run:

```text
python src/studio.py --price-csv YOUR.csv --as-of 2025-12-31 --out results/custom-study.json
python src/studio.py --price-csv YOUR.csv --as-of 2025-12-31 --check results/custom-study.json
python lab.py --price-csv YOUR.csv --as-of 2025-12-31 --output results/custom-results.json
```

Write the custom studio output to `docs/study.json` to view it in the existing
dashboard. The new **Calibrated scenarios** selector shows dates, comparison
metrics, charts, model values and numerical sensitivity. The data export
includes the complete fitted parameter snapshot and replicate changes.

## Actual findings

Measured with Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.2.4 and SciPy 1.15.2 on CPU, as of
2025-12-31. Values below are USD, rounded from the recorded artifacts.

| Parameter set | Initial book value | 99% VaR | 99% ES | 99% PFE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frozen synthetic | 652.94 | 683.88 | 764.17 | 1,541.60 |
| CSV spots, frozen volatility/correlation | 105.02 | 333.05 | 380.42 | 410.19 |
| CSV calibrated | 141.08 | 243.98 | 277.37 | 378.77 |

Fitted annual volatilities are approximately [0.280036, 0.198049, 0.271056,
0.397916]. Several are higher than the frozen inputs, yet fitted VaR and ES
are lower. The short put, long positions, spot moneyness and correlations
jointly determine this book's response; higher individual volatilities do not
imply a higher portfolio risk metric in this joint comparison. The experiment
does not separately attribute the volatility and correlation effects.

| Calibrated minus spot-matched | Seed 1729 change | 12-scramble mean change | SD across scrambles | Numerical SE of mean |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| VaR | −89.0633 | −89.2971 | 2.0464 | 0.5908 |
| ES | −103.0445 | −103.3021 | 0.3241 | 0.0936 |
| PFE | −31.4162 | −32.0035 | 0.9392 | 0.2711 |

All twelve paired changes are negative for each metric. Lower estimates are
retained without describing them as safer or better calibrated. The model has
no held-out accuracy target here. Comparing only the first and third rows would
confound the substantial spot move with the fitted covariance change.

All **15 unit tests pass**. They independently reconstruct sample covariance,
exercise malformed inputs and cutoff behavior, permute asset columns, replay
the engine from calibrated parameters, reject unsupported books and invalid
matrices, reconstruct comparison summaries, and retain original numerical
baselines. All **four studio cases reproduce**, including the original risk,
stress and amplitude examples. Lab artifacts preserve the repeated-call
convergence study from QE01 alongside the new portfolio comparison.
A [validation receipt](QE02-validation.json) also records successful custom-CSV
CLI reproduction with an earlier cutoff, rejection of a missing cutoff,
comparison of all three legacy cases against the original artifact, and the
trusted contract checks.

## Limitations

No real market observations or real-world accuracy are claimed. The adapter
assumes consecutive synchronized adjusted daily closes, 252 equal trading
steps per year and no missing observations; date spacing is not converted into
elapsed-time weights. Holidays, splits, dividends and stale quotes need user
review. Nonzero return variance and diagonal shrinkage regularize the matrix
but do not establish stable future covariance.

The 3% scenario drift remains risk-neutral in form. These are illustrative
model distributions, not validated physical loss probabilities. Historical
volatility is not an implied-volatility surface; transaction costs, stochastic
volatility, defaults, liquidity and funding remain omitted. Gaussian GBM loses
heavy tails, skewness and clustering. Parameter uncertainty, shrinkage choice,
window sensitivity and out-of-sample validation are unmeasured. The scramble
standard errors cannot quantify those model errors. Tail quantities remain
finite-sample estimates; no tail-coverage guarantee, novel estimator or quantum
advantage follows from this study.

## What the author should understand for an interview

- Explain how the as-of cutoff prevents future-price leakage, and distinguish
  the cutoff from the last available close and from a synthetic assumption date.
- Derive log-return covariance, N−1 normalization, annualization and diagonal
  shrinkage; defend them as assumptions rather than universally optimal choices.
- Explain the asset-name mapping and why changing column order must not alter
  portfolio contracts or simulated losses.
- Separate spot sensitivity from fitted covariance sensitivity, and explain
  why the initial book is repriced under each volatility proxy.
- Distinguish numerical variability across Sobol scrambles from historical
  estimation uncertainty and model misspecification. Calibration can lower a
  risk estimate without making it more accurate.
- Describe which new evidence a non-Gaussian or physical risk model would need.
  The portfolio comparison remains separate from the discrete quantum oracle.
