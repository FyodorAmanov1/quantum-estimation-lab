# QE05: Amplitude interval coverage

## Question

At the bounded option-book oracle's fixed query budget, do the nominal 95%
likelihood intervals contain the exact encoded expectation across independent
measurement trials? What happens at zero exposure, full capped exposure and
near-boundary amplitudes? Compare a classical binomial interval at equal
logical preparation cost and retain the existing estimator's failures.

## Method

Freeze the QE04 empirical exposure laws: the CSV-calibrated synthetic four-asset
book under GBM and the specified variance mixture, 32768 scenarios, seed 1729,
400 USD cap and 32 floor-rounded exposure levels. Their exact amplitudes are
0.3491673623361895 and 0.34888187531502013. These are conditional histogram
truths, already known classically, rather than population exposure truths.
User CSV inputs propagate through the same existing bounded-exposure case.

Test these two amplitudes plus 0, 0.000001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.5, 0.99, 0.999,
0.999999 and 1. Each target has 128 independent measurement trials, giving
1408 amplified experiments. Every trial uses powers k=(0,1,2,4,8), 256 shots
per power: 1280 observations and sum_k 256*(2*k+1)=8960 preparation/inverse
calls. No adaptive stopping or changes in allocation are made.

Explicit Householder/Grover matrix evolution is checked against
p_k=sin²((2k+1)arcsin(sqrt(a))) for every target. Repeated trials then sample
the corresponding ideal binomial law without repeating dense matrix powers.
Exact boundary laws are set to zero or one to avoid floating-point leakage.
Root SeedSequence 2505 spawns target/trial/method streams in order, with
separate uint64 seeds for amplified and classical measurements. Independence
is the practical pseudorandom-stream design, not a mathematical RNG proof.
Every seed and count is recorded in the JSON artifacts.

Compare three procedures:

- **Legacy:** unchanged public estimator's 32769-point theta mesh from 1e-6
  to pi/2-1e-6, clipped interior probabilities and log-likelihood drop 1.920729.
- **Endpoint-inclusive:** the same counts, interior mesh and fixed cutoff,
  adding theta=0 and pi/2 with exact endpoint likelihoods. This is an inference
  diagnostic, not a replacement of the public estimator or a calibrated cutoff.
- **Classical exact:** 8960 independent power-zero Bernoulli observations,
  with the equal-tailed 95% Clopper-Pearson interval, implemented by beta
  quantiles and independently tested against SciPy binomtest.

[Suzuki et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10246) give the multi-power
maximum-likelihood algorithmic background. [Clopper and Pearson (1934)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404)
give the binomial confidence-limit construction. Neither citation validates
finite-sample coverage of this mesh-based likelihood procedure.

Report interval-hull coverage, bias, RMSE, mean width and exact binomial 95%
Monte Carlo uncertainty intervals for each coverage fraction. A likelihood
set can be disconnected, so direct likelihood membership at the truth and
the number of mesh components are recorded separately. Legacy membership
excludes the exact boundaries because they lie outside its search domain.
Hull endpoints are mesh points, with no interpolation of threshold crossings.

python src/studio.py --out docs/study.json
python src/studio.py --check docs/study.json

YYYY-MM-DD` to the lab and studio commands; the boundary diagnostics stay fixed
while both empirical book targets change.

## Actual findings

Counts below are measured from [lab results](../results/latest.json) and
[studio data](../docs/study.json). Every denominator is 128; nominal coverage
is 95%. The two likelihood methods share counts and must not be treated as
independent experiments.

| Amplitude / target | Legacy hits | Endpoint-inclusive hits | Classical exact hits |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0 | 128 | 128 |
| 0.000001 | 127 | 127 | 127 |
| 0.001 | 120 | 120 | 124 |
| 0.01 | 120 | 120 | 124 |
| 0.5 | 123 | 123 | 124 |
| 0.99 | 119 | 119 | 120 |
| 0.999 | 124 | 124 | 123 |
| 0.999999 | 128 | 128 | 127 |
| 1 | 0 | 128 | 128 |
| GBM empirical exposure | 119 | 119 | 119 |
| Mixture empirical exposure | 123 | 123 | 122 |

The legacy interval fails deterministically at both exact boundaries because
its search excludes them, despite point errors near 1e-12. Endpoint inclusion
repairs this exclusion. Across 128 trials, the Monte Carlo interval for 0/128
coverage is [0,2.84%], and for 128/128 is [97.16%,100%]. Near-boundary coverage
is highly discrete; endpoint inclusion does not establish nominal 95%
calibration elsewhere.

For GBM, likelihood coverage is 92.96875%, with Monte Carlo interval
[87.07%,96.74%]; for the mixture it is 96.09375%, with interval
[91.12%,98.72%]. These uncertainties include 95%; the experiment does not
establish systematic undercoverage or exact calibration for the book targets.
GBM classical coverage is also 119/128; mixture classical coverage is 122/128.

| Conditional amplitude statistic | GBM likelihood | GBM classical | Mixture likelihood | Mixture classical |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bias | +0.00021671 | +0.00035482 | +0.00008648 | -0.00000492 |
| RMSE | 0.00163487 | 0.00537047 | 0.00152844 | 0.00504297 |
| Mean interval width | 0.00580414 | 0.01985361 | 0.00580862 | 0.01984532 |

Multiply these statistics by 400 for conditional USD units. Thus GBM
likelihood RMSE is about 0.654 USD and classical RMSE 2.148 USD, against the
same known grid truth. This repeated-trial ranking differs from the two
unfavorable realized trials in QE04, which remain preserved. Neither result
establishes general superiority, runtime speedup or population-risk accuracy.

At a=0.5, direct likelihood-set membership covers 124 trials while the
mesh-hull interval covers 123: numerical threshold edges can exclude truth
between accepted mesh nodes. At a=0.01 and 0.99, three trials each have
disconnected accepted mesh sets. These diagnostics make the numerical and
topological limitations observable; the study does not silently interpolate
or claim connected likelihood sets.

## Costs and limitations

Each sampled method uses 12,615,680 logical preparation calls across the full
study. The two likelihood procedures reuse one measurement experiment and
incur no extra oracle calls. The classical baseline has 8960 measured shots
per trial, versus 1280 amplified shots. Equal preparation calls do not imply
equal circuit depth, gate count, runtime or data-loading cost. Inference,
classical scenario revaluation, matrix preparation, compilation and noise
are excluded. Existing QE04 scenario preprocessing is reused.

These are synthetic inputs and ideal measurements. Coverage is conditional
on a frozen empirical capped/grid law, so it says nothing about truncation,
discretization, scenario, parameter or model uncertainty. It does not cover
uncapped population mean exposure, PFE, VaR, CVA or market losses. The finite
mesh and asymptotic cutoff have no finite-sample guarantee; adding endpoints
does not fix cutoff calibration or mesh resolution. Monte Carlo uncertainty
is per target, without a multiple-comparison adjustment. A complete exact
histogram expectation is already available before quantum sampling; no
efficient coherent pricing oracle or quantum advantage is demonstrated.

## What the author should understand for an interview

- Derive p_k and the 2k+1 preparation/inverse accounting; distinguish shots
  from preparation calls and explain the classical comparator's access.
- Explain why an almost exact point estimate can have zero interval coverage
  at the support boundary, and why adding endpoints is not coverage calibration.
- Distinguish a likelihood set, its bounding interval, numerical mesh edges
  and actual repeated-trial coverage. Explain the a=0.5 discrepancy.
- Replay counts from an exported child seed and reconstruct coverage, bias,
  RMSE and Monte Carlo uncertainty from the raw trials.
- Separate conditional measurement uncertainty from QE04's cap/grid errors,
  scenario sampling and model risk. Explain why the known histogram baseline
  prevents a computational advantage claim from this implementation.
